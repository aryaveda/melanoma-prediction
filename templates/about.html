About This Project & Methodology

The Challenge: Skin Cancer Classification

Skin cancer, particularly melanoma, is a significant health concern worldwide. Early detection drastically improves prognosis, but accurate visual diagnosis can be challenging even for experienced dermatologists. Automated systems using deep learning offer potential to assist clinicians by providing objective analysis of dermoscopic images.

This project addresses the challenge of classifying skin lesions into multiple categories (including melanoma and various benign types) using dermoscopic images and associated patient metadata.

Model Architecture

The core of this tool is a hybrid deep learning model designed to leverage both visual information from images and contextual information from patient metadata:

  • Convolutional Neural Network (CNN): An EfficientNet (specifically, EfficientNet-B5) acts as the primary image feature extractor. EfficientNets are known for their balance of accuracy and computational efficiency. It processes the input image to capture visual patterns and textures relevant to lesion classification.
  • Vision Transformer (ViT): A small Vision Transformer operates in parallel with the CNN. ViTs process images by dividing them into patches and analyzing relationships between these patches, capturing global context that might be missed by the CNN.
  • Feature Fusion: Features extracted by the CNN and ViT are concatenated and passed through a fusion layer (a Multi-Layer Perceptron - MLP) to create a combined visual representation.
  • Metadata Integration (Conditional): If enabled (USE_METADATA=True in the configuration), patient metadata (age, sex, lesion location) is processed through a separate pathway involving an Attention Mechanism. This allows the model to weigh the importance of different metadata features. The processed metadata representation is then added to the fused visual features.
  • Classifier: The final combined feature vector is fed into a classifier (another MLP) to produce the probability scores for each skin lesion category.

This hybrid approach aims to capture both fine-grained local details (CNN) and broader global context (ViT), enhanced by relevant patient information when available.

Dataset(s)

The model was trained primarily on the ISIC Challenge datasets from 2017, 2018, 2019, and 2020. These datasets consist of dermoscopic images of skin lesions, along with associated patient metadata (like age, sex, and lesion location) where available.

A variety of data augmentation techniques were applied during training to improve model robustness and generalization. These included:

  • Geometric Transformations: Flips (horizontal, vertical), transpose, shifts, scaling, rotations, perspective changes.
  • Color & Lighting Adjustments: Brightness/contrast changes, hue/saturation/value shifts, channel shuffling, CLAHE (contrast limited adaptive histogram equalization), gamma correction.
  • Distortion & Noise: Elastic transforms, grid/optical distortions, motion/median/gaussian blur, gaussian noise.
  • Occlusion & Texture Simulation: Coarse dropout (simulating occlusions), superpixels, sharpening/embossing, random shadows.

These augmentations help the model learn invariant features and perform better on unseen images with variations in appearance, lighting, or quality.

Key Techniques

  • Progressive Unfreezing: The training process involved initially freezing the early layers of the pre-trained EfficientNet backbone and gradually unfreezing them epoch by epoch. This allows the model to first adapt the final layers to the specific task before fine-tuning the deeper, more general feature extractors, often leading to more stable training.
  • Weighted Loss Function: A weighted Cross-Entropy loss was used to address class imbalance inherent in skin lesion datasets. Classes with fewer samples were given higher weights, preventing the model from being biased towards common classes like nevi. An auxiliary Binary Cross-Entropy loss specifically for melanoma detection was also added.
  • Dynamic Dropout Scaling: Dropout rates for different parts of the model were adjusted dynamically during training based on the EfficientNet version (B3-B7) and the current training phase. Larger models generally received less dropout penalty compared to smaller ones.
  • Model Calibration (Temperature Scaling): After training, temperature scaling was applied to the model's output logits. This post-processing step adjusts the output probabilities to better reflect the true confidence of the predictions, making them more reliable.
  • Grad-CAM Visualization: Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) is employed to generate heatmaps. These visualizations highlight the image regions most influential in the model's prediction for a specific class, offering insights into the model's decision-making process.
